Ranking substrate preference for *Pseudomonas* (specifically the model organism *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*) is based on the phenomenon of **Carbon Catabolite Repression (CCR)**. Unlike *E. coli*, which prefers glucose, *Pseudomonas* evolved to prioritize organic acids and specific amino acids that enter the TCA cycle directly.

This ranking is derived from metabolic studies (e.g., Palmer et al., Frimmersdorf et al.) and the known hierarchy of the Crc (Catabolite Repression Control) system.

### The Ranking (Highest Preference to Lowest)

1.  **Glutamate** (The "gold standard" amino acid; enters TCA directly via $\alpha$-ketoglutarate)
2.  **Proline** (Rapidly converted to glutamate)
3.  **Arginine** (Converted to glutamate/succinate; highly preferred)
4.  **Alanine** (Converted directly to pyruvate)
5.  **Serine** (Converted directly to pyruvate)
6.  **4-guanidinobutanoic acid** (An intermediate of arginine catabolism; highly preferred)
7.  **Glucose** (The most preferred sugar, but repressed by the amino acids above)
8.  **Mannitol** (A sugar alcohol utilized efficiently via the Entner-Doudoroff pathway)
9.  **Leucine** (Branched-chain amino acid; enters via Acetyl-CoA)
10. **Isoleucine** (Branched-chain amino acid; enters via Succinyl-CoA)
11. **Phenylalanine** (Aromatic amino acid; requires more complex degradation)
12. **Lysine** (Slowly metabolized compared to other amino acids)
13. **Threonine** (Metabolism is slower and often secondary to other carbon sources)
14. **Glycine** (Often used as a nitrogen source rather than a primary carbon source)
15. **Fructose** (Utilized, but glucose is generally preferred among hexoses)
16. **Mannose** (Enters glycolysis/ED pathway but less efficiently than glucose)
17. **Adenosine** (Nucleoside; carbon is derived from the ribose moiety)
18. **Uridine** (Nucleoside; carbon is derived from the ribose moiety)
19. **Guanine** (Purine base; primarily a nitrogen source, poor carbon source)
20. **Hypoxanthine** (Purine base; primarily a nitrogen source)
21. **Trehalose** (Disaccharide; utilization is strain-dependent and often slow)
22. **Maltose** (Disaccharide; *P. aeruginosa* lacks efficient transport for many complex sugars)
23. **Arabinose** (Pentose; most *P. aeruginosa* strains cannot utilize D-arabinose as a sole carbon source)

---

### Key Principles of this Ranking:

*   **TCA Cycle Proximity:** *Pseudomonas* prefers substrates that require the fewest enzymatic steps to reach the TCA cycle. Glutamate, Proline, and Arginine are "top tier" because they feed directly into the $\alpha$-ketoglutarate node.
*   **Reverse CCR:** In *Pseudomonas*, the presence of preferred amino acids (like Glutamate) actually represses the genes for glucose metabolism (*glk*, *edd*, *gap*). This is the opposite of the hierarchy seen in enteric bacteria.
*   **Sugar Limitations:** *Pseudomonas* lacks the Phosphotransferase System (PTS) for many sugars. It uses the Entner-Doudoroff (ED) pathway rather than the standard Embden-Meyerhof-Parnas (EMP) glycolysis, making sugars less energetically favorable than organic acids.
*   **Nucleosides and Bases:** While *Pseudomonas* can salvage carbon from the ribose sugar in nucleosides (Adenosine/Uridine), the nitrogenous bases (Guanine/Hypoxanthine) are very poor carbon sources and are almost exclusively used for nitrogen or nucleotide recycling.
