Ranking the substrate preference of *Pseudomonas* (specifically the model organism *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*) requires understanding its unique metabolic hierarchy. Unlike *E. coli*, which prefers glucose, *Pseudomonas* exhibits **reverse Carbon Catabolite Repression (CCR)**, meaning it prefers organic acids and certain amino acids over sugars.

This ranking is based on growth rates, oxygen consumption rates, and the hierarchy established by the Catabolite Repression Control (Crc) system in *P. aeruginosa* PAO1.

### The Ranking (Highest to Lowest Preference)

1.  **Glutamate** (The "gold standard" carbon/nitrogen source for *Pseudomonas*)
2.  **Proline** (Highly preferred; enters the TCA cycle rapidly)
3.  **Arginine** (Major source of C and N; high uptake priority)
4.  **Alanine** (Easily converted to pyruvate)
5.  **Serine** (Rapidly utilized via conversion to pyruvate)
6.  **Glucose** (The most preferred sugar, but repressed by the amino acids above)
7.  **Mannitol** (Excellent polyol source; often used as a primary carbon source)
8.  **Fructose** (Utilized well via the Entner-Doudoroff pathway)
9.  **Phenylalanine** (Strongly utilized via the homogentisate pathway)
10. **Leucine** (Branched-chain amino acid; good growth yield)
11. **Isoleucine** (Similar to leucine, slightly lower priority)
12. **4-guanidinobutanoic acid** (An intermediate in arginine catabolism; well-utilized)
13. **Adenosine** (Preferred nucleoside; used as both C and N source)
14. **Uridine** (Utilized efficiently, though slightly less than adenosine)
15. **Mannose** (Utilized, but often requires induction of specific isomerases)
16. **Lysine** (Catabolism is complex and slower than other amino acids)
17. **Threonine** (Lower priority; catabolic pathways are less efficient in *Pseudomonas*)
18. **Glycine** (Often used as a nitrogen source rather than a primary carbon source)
19. **Guanine** (Purine base; utilized slowly for carbon)
20. **Hypoxanthine** (Salvage pathway substrate; poor carbon source)
21. **Trehalose** (Disaccharide; *Pseudomonas* lacks high-efficiency uptake for this compared to monomers)
22. **Maltose** (Very poor growth substrate for most *P. aeruginosa* strains)
23. **Arabinose** (Most *P. aeruginosa* strains lack the functional *ara* operon and cannot utilize L-arabinose)

---

### Rationale for the Hierarchy

#### 1. The Amino Acid Preference (Top Tier)
*Pseudomonas* is an environmental opportunist that evolved in environments rich in decaying organic matter and protein. **Glutamate, Proline, and Arginine** are ranked at the top because they serve as dual carbon and nitrogen sources and enter the TCA cycle with very few enzymatic steps. In a mixture of glucose and glutamate, *Pseudomonas* will actually consume the glutamate first.

#### 2. The Sugar Paradox (Middle Tier)
While glucose is the "king" for many bacteria, in *Pseudomonas*, the **Entner-Doudoroff (ED) pathway** is the primary route for sugar metabolism (they lack a functional glycolysis/EMD pathway). **Glucose and Mannitol** are the most efficient of the carbohydrates, but they are subject to repression by the Crc protein if preferred amino acids are present.

#### 3. Nucleosides vs. Bases (Lower-Middle Tier)
**Adenosine and Uridine** are ranked higher than their base counterparts (**Guanine/Hypoxanthine**) because the ribose sugar component provides an immediate carbon source, while the nitrogenous base is primarily used for nitrogen salvage.

#### 4. The "Poor" Substrates (Bottom Tier)
*   **Glycine/Threonine:** These are often metabolized slowly because their breakdown can lead to the accumulation of toxic intermediates (like glyoxylate or methylglyoxal) if not tightly regulated.
*   **Disaccharides (Maltose/Trehalose):** *Pseudomonas* species generally lack the high-affinity phosphotransferase systems (PTS) that enteric bacteria use to "scavenge" complex sugars.
*   **Arabinose:** This is a 5-carbon sugar that *P. aeruginosa* PAO1 famously cannot utilize, though some other species in the genus (like *P. putida*) might.
