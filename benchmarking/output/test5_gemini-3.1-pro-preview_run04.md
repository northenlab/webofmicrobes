Ranking the metabolic preference of substrates for the *Pseudomonas* genus (with *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and *Pseudomonas putida* as the primary models) requires understanding their unique **Carbon Catabolite Repression (CCR)** system. 

Unlike *E. coli*, which prefers glucose over almost everything else, ***Pseudomonas* exhibits "reverse" catabolite repression**. They highly prefer organic acids and specific amino acids over sugars. Sugars are only utilized when these preferred amino acids are depleted. Furthermore, *Pseudomonas* species have a very restricted ability to utilize complex sugars.

Here is the ranking of the 23 substrates from highest to lowest preference, categorized into metabolic tiers for clarity:

### Tier 1: The Highly Preferred Amino Acids (Strong CCR Inducers)
These substrates feed directly into the TCA cycle or are converted to pyruvate. They are consumed first, and their presence actively represses the utilization of sugars and other amino acids.
**1. Glutamate** *(The absolute most preferred carbon/nitrogen source for Pseudomonas)*
**2. Proline** *(Rapidly oxidized directly into glutamate)*
**3. Arginine** *(Rapidly catabolized into glutamate via the AST pathway)*
**4. Alanine** *(Directly converted to pyruvate)*

### Tier 2: Secondary Amino Acids & Pathway Intermediates
These are excellent carbon and nitrogen sources, utilized very efficiently, but generally secondary to the "Tier 1" group.
**5. 4-guanidinobutanoic acid** *(A direct intermediate in the preferred arginine catabolism pathway)*
**6. Serine** *(Easily deaminated to pyruvate)*
**7. Threonine** *(Readily cleaved into glycine and acetyl-CoA)*

### Tier 3: Complex, Branched, and Aromatic Amino Acids
These require more complex, multi-step catabolic pathways (e.g., the Liu pathway for branched-chain amino acids) and yield slower growth rates than Tier 1 and 2.
**8. Leucine** 
**9. Isoleucine**
**10. Phenylalanine**
**11. Lysine**
**12. Glycine** *(While usable, it is often utilized more for C1 metabolism and purine synthesis than as a bulk carbon energy source)*

### Tier 4: The Preferred Sugars and Sugar Alcohols
*Pseudomonas* lacks glycolysis (EMP pathway) and relies on the Entner-Doudoroff (ED) pathway. These sugars are utilized well, but **only after** the preferred amino acids (Tiers 1-3) have been exhausted.
**13. Glucose** *(The most preferred sugar, but still subordinate to amino acids)*
**14. Fructose**
**15. Mannitol**

### Tier 5: Nucleosides and Nucleobases
*Pseudomonas* can salvage and catabolize purines and pyrimidines for carbon and nitrogen, but this requires specific induction and yields slower growth compared to primary amino acids or simple sugars. Nucleosides (which contain a ribose sugar) are generally preferred over free nucleobases.
**16. Adenosine** *(Nucleoside)*
**17. Uridine** *(Nucleoside)*
**18. Hypoxanthine** *(Nucleobase)*
**19. Guanine** *(Nucleobase)*

### Tier 6: Poorly Utilized or Non-Utilized Sugars
*Pseudomonas* species are famously non-fermentative and have a highly restricted sugar diet. They generally lack the specific porins, transporters, or kinases required to utilize these sugars. In standard clinical and environmental biochemical tests, these are almost always negative.
**20. Mannose** *(Some strains can utilize it very slowly, but it is highly inefficient)*
**21. Trehalose** *(Rarely utilized by most major Pseudomonas species)*
**22. Arabinose** *(Generally negative for P. aeruginosa, though a few environmental strains may show weak growth)*
**23. Maltose** *(Universally recognized as a non-utilized substrate for P. aeruginosa and most major Pseudomonas species; they lack the transport mechanisms for this disaccharide).*

***

### Summary of the Biological Rationale:
1. **The CbrAB/Crc System:** This is the regulatory network in *Pseudomonas* that enforces the preference for **Glutamate > other Amino Acids > Glucose**. 
2. **Arginine/Proline:** These rank at the very top because *Pseudomonas* possesses highly efficient pathways to turn them immediately into Glutamate.
3. **Sugar Exclusion:** Because *Pseudomonas* evolved in soil and aquatic environments rich in decaying organic matter (proteins/lipids) rather than free sugars, their metabolism is hardwired to ignore complex sugars (like maltose and arabinose) entirely.
