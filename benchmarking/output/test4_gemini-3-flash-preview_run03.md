To selectively enrich *Streptomyces* while suppressing *Pseudomonas*, you must exploit the fundamental ecological differences between them. *Pseudomonas* are "r-strategists"—fast-growing generalists that thrive on simple, soluble nutrients. *Streptomyces* are "K-strategists"—slower-growing specialists that excel at degrading complex, insoluble polymers.

The most effective strategy is to provide **complex carbon sources that require extracellular enzymatic degradation**, which *Streptomyces* are evolutionarily optimized for, but which *Pseudomonas* struggle to utilize efficiently.

Here are the best metabolites and substrates for this purpose:

### 1. Chitin (The Gold Standard)
Chitin is a polymer of N-acetylglucosamine found in fungal cell walls and arthropod exoskeletons.
*   **Why it works:** *Streptomyces* are among the most prolific producers of chitinases in soil. They use their filamentous growth to penetrate chitin particles. While some *Pseudomonas* species possess chitinases, they are rarely as efficient as *Streptomyces* in utilizing insoluble chitin as a primary carbon and nitrogen source.
*   **Application:** Use colloidal chitin or finely ground shrimp shells. In lab settings, "Chitin Agar" is the standard medium for isolating *Streptomyces* from soil.

### 2. Starch (Amylose and Amylopectin)
*   **Why it works:** Most *Streptomyces* produce potent extracellular $\alpha$-amylases to break down starch into maltose and glucose. While *Pseudomonas* can use glucose, many species (like *P. putida*) are surprisingly poor at degrading raw, complex starches compared to actinomycetes.
*   **Application:** Use soluble starch as the primary carbon source. This is why International Streptomyces Project (ISP) Medium 4 (Inorganic Salts-Starch Agar) is so effective.

### 3. Xylan and Hemicellulose
*   **Why it works:** *Streptomyces* possess a vast toolkit of hemicellulases (xylanases). *Pseudomonas* species are generally optimized for organic acids and simple sugars; they often lack the robust machinery to break down the complex branched structure of xylan found in plant cell walls.
*   **Application:** Supplementing soil or media with birchwood or oat spelt xylan.

### 4. Inulin
*   **Why it works:** Inulin is a fructose polymer. Many *Streptomyces* species produce inulinases. While some *Pseudomonas* can grow on it, it is not a preferred substrate for them, and they are often outcompeted by the high-affinity uptake systems of actinomycetes in an inulin-rich environment.

### 5. Specific Nitrogen Sources: Asparagine and Casein
*   **Why it works:** *Streptomyces* have a high affinity for organic nitrogen. Asparagine is the classic nitrogen source used in Czapek-Dox or Asparagine-Starch media to favor actinomycetes.
*   **Application:** Use L-asparagine as the sole nitrogen source.

---

### What to AVOID (Metabolites that promote *Pseudomonas*)
If you want to suppress *Pseudomonas*, you must minimize or eliminate the following:
*   **Simple Sugars:** Glucose, Sucrose, and Fructose. *Pseudomonas* will rapidly outgrow *Streptomyces* on these.
*   **Organic Acids:** Citrate, Malate, Succinate, and Acetate. These are the "preferred" carbon sources for the *Pseudomonas* TCA cycle and will lead to a population explosion.
*   **High Phosphorus:** *Pseudomonas* thrives in high-phosphate environments. Keeping phosphorus levels moderate can help *Streptomyces*.

---

### Complementary Selective Pressures
Metabolites alone may not be enough because *Pseudomonas* can "scavenge" the breakdown products (like glucose) produced by *Streptomyces* enzymes. To ensure success, combine the metabolites above with these conditions:

1.  **pH Adjustment:** Aim for a **pH of 8.0 to 8.5**. *Streptomyces* are highly tolerant of (and prefer) alkaline conditions, whereas many soil *Pseudomonas* prefer neutral to slightly acidic pH. Use Calcium Carbonate ($CaCO_3$) to buffer the soil/media to an alkaline state.
2.  **Water Potential:** *Streptomyces* are more resistant to desiccation due to their spores. Allowing the soil to undergo **slight drying cycles** will hit the moisture-loving *Pseudomonas* hard while triggering *Streptomyces* sporulation and survival.
3.  **Temperature:** Incubate at **30°C**. While both grow here, this temperature combined with complex polymers favors the enzymatic rate of *Streptomyces*.
4.  **Chemical Selection (If using Agar):**
    *   **Nalidixic Acid:** Inhibits Gram-negative bacteria like *Pseudomonas* but *Streptomyces* are often resistant.
    *   **Cycloheximide:** Essential to prevent fungi from eating the chitin/starch you added.
