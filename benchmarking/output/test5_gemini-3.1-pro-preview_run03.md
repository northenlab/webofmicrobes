Ranking the metabolic preference of substrates for *Pseudomonas* (particularly well-studied models like *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and *Pseudomonas putida*) requires understanding a fundamental quirk of their biology: **Reverse Carbon Catabolite Repression (CCR)**. 

Unlike *E. coli*, which prefers glucose over everything else, *Pseudomonas* species strongly prefer organic acids and specific amino acids over sugars. When preferred amino acids are present, the bacteria will actively repress the genes required to consume sugars and other less efficient substrates.

Assuming these are provided as sole carbon/energy sources, here is the ranking of the 23 substrates from highest to lowest preference, followed by the biochemical rationale.

### The Ranking (Highest to Lowest Preference)

**Tier 1: The "Elite" Amino Acids (Direct TCA Cycle Entry)**
1. **Glutamate** *(The absolute most preferred substrate; represses almost all other pathways)*
2. **Proline** *(Rapidly converted directly into glutamate)*
3. **Arginine** *(Highly preferred; converted to glutamate via the AST pathway)*
4. **Alanine** *(Converted directly to pyruvate)*
5. **Serine** *(Converted directly to pyruvate)*

**Tier 2: Secondary Amino Acids & Intermediates**
6. **4-guanidinobutanoic acid** *(A direct intermediate in the *Pseudomonas* arginine degradation pathway; highly preferred but slightly less efficient than pure arginine)*
7. **Leucine** *(Branched-chain; feeds efficiently into acetyl-CoA)*
8. **Isoleucine** *(Branched-chain; feeds into acetyl-CoA and succinyl-CoA)*
9. **Phenylalanine** *(Aromatic; degraded efficiently via the homogentisate or catechol pathways)*

**Tier 3: The Preferred Sugars (Entner-Doudoroff Pathway)**
10. **Glucose** *(The most preferred sugar, but its consumption is repressed by Tiers 1 & 2)*
11. **Mannitol** *(A preferred sugar alcohol; feeds easily into fructose metabolism)*
12. **Fructose** *(Feeds directly into the Entner-Doudoroff pathway)*

**Tier 4: Lesser Amino Acids**
13. **Threonine** *(Slower degradation into glycine and acetyl-CoA)*
14. **Lysine** *(Requires a complex, multi-step degradation pathway)*
15. **Glycine** *(A very poor sole carbon source; better utilized as a nitrogen source)*

**Tier 5: Nucleosides and Nucleobases**
16. **Adenosine** *(Ribose sugar attached provides some carbon; adenine used for salvage)*
17. **Uridine** *(Ribose sugar provides carbon; uracil used for salvage)*
18. **Hypoxanthine** *(Free purine base; poor carbon source, used mostly for nitrogen/salvage)*
19. **Guanine** *(Free purine base; poor carbon source, used mostly for nitrogen/salvage)*

**Tier 6: Poor, Stress-Related, or Non-Metabolized Sugars**
20. **Trehalose** *(Metabolized by some strains, but usually synthesized internally as an osmotic stress protectant rather than preferred as food)*
21. **Mannose** *(Very poorly utilized; strain-dependent)*
22. **Arabinose** *(Most *Pseudomonas* species lack the native pathways to utilize this pentose sugar)*
23. **Maltose** *(Generally not utilized at all; *Pseudomonas* lacks the transporters and maltase enzymes. Being "maltose-negative" is a classic diagnostic trait).*

---

### Biochemical Rationale for this Ranking

**1. Why are Glutamate, Proline, and Arginine at the top?**
*Pseudomonas* metabolism is optimized to feed the Tricarboxylic Acid (TCA) cycle directly. Glutamate is converted in one step to $\alpha$-ketoglutarate, a core TCA intermediate. Proline and Arginine are highly preferred because *Pseudomonas* possesses incredibly efficient, rapid pathways to turn them both into Glutamate. When these are present, the Crc/Hfq regulatory system shuts down the transport and metabolism of sugars.

**2. Why are sugars ranked so low?**
*Pseudomonas* lacks the standard glycolysis pathway (Embden-Meyerhof-Parnas). Instead, they use the Entner-Doudoroff (ED) pathway. While they will happily eat glucose, mannitol, and fructose if nothing else is available, they will ignore them if amino acids are present. 

**3. Why are Nucleosides/Bases near the bottom?**
While *Pseudomonas* can scavenge adenosine, uridine, guanine, and hypoxanthine, these are primarily viewed by the cell as sources of Nitrogen and building blocks for DNA/RNA (salvage pathways). The nucleosides (Adenosine, Uridine) rank slightly higher than the free bases (Guanine, Hypoxanthine) because the attached ribose sugar can be stripped off and used for carbon energy.

**4. Why are Arabinose and Maltose dead last?**
*Pseudomonas* species are largely incapable of eating complex or non-native sugars. They do not possess the genetic machinery to transport or break down maltose (a glucose disaccharide) or arabinose (a 5-carbon sugar) without genetic engineering.
