Ranking substrate preference for *Pseudomonas* (specifically the model organism *P. aeruginosa*) is unique because, unlike *E. coli*, *Pseudomonas* prefers organic acids and specific amino acids over glucose. This phenomenon is known as **Reverse Catabolite Repression**.

The following ranking is based on growth rates, carbon catabolite repression (CCR) hierarchies, and the efficiency of the Entner-Doudoroff (ED) pathway versus the TCA cycle.

### **Tier 1: The Preferred Substrates (TCA Cycle Precursors)**
These substrates are utilized first and will actively repress the metabolism of sugars.
1.  **Glutamate** (The primary carbon and nitrogen source for *Pseudomonas*)
2.  **Arginine** (Highly preferred; enters the TCA cycle via the AST pathway)
3.  **Proline** (Rapidly converted to glutamate)
4.  **4-guanidinobutanoic acid** (An intermediate in arginine/putrescine degradation; highly prioritized)
5.  **Alanine** (Easily converted to pyruvate)

### **Tier 2: High-Efficiency Amino Acids and Primary Sugars**
6.  **Serine** (Directly converted to pyruvate, though slightly less stable than alanine)
7.  **Glucose** (The most preferred sugar, but its uptake is repressed by Tier 1 substrates)
8.  **Mannitol** (A polyol that *Pseudomonas* utilizes very efficiently via the ED pathway)
9.  **Fructose** (Enters the ED pathway; often utilized nearly as fast as glucose)
10. **Mannose** (Utilized well, but requires more processing than glucose/fructose)

### **Tier 3: Secondary Amino Acids and Complex Sugars**
11. **Threonine** (Enters via glycine/acetyl-CoA pathways)
12. **Glycine** (Used readily, but less energy-dense than others)
13. **Leucine** (Branched-chain amino acid; degradation is complex but efficient)
14. **Isoleucine** (Similar to leucine, slightly slower catabolism)
15. **Phenylalanine** (Aromatic degradation is energy-intensive but effective in *Pseudomonas*)
16. **Lysine** (Degradation is relatively slow compared to other amino acids)

### **Tier 4: Nucleosides and Low-Preference Sugars**
17. **Adenosine** (Can be used as a carbon source, but primarily a nitrogen/purine source)
18. **Uridine** (Similar to adenosine; ribose sugar is used, but less preferred than free glucose)
19. **Trehalose** (Utilization varies by strain; often requires induction of specific alpha-glucosidases)
20. **Maltose** (Generally a poor substrate for *P. aeruginosa* compared to other *Pseudomonads*)
21. **Arabinose** (Many *P. aeruginosa* strains, including PAO1, lack the full pathway to use this efficiently as a sole carbon source)

### **Tier 5: Purine Bases (Poor Carbon Sources)**
These are primarily used for nitrogen salvage rather than as primary carbon/energy sources.
22. **Guanine** (Very low solubility and slow catabolic rate for carbon)
23. **Hypoxanthine** (Last in preference; used almost exclusively for the purine salvage pathway)
