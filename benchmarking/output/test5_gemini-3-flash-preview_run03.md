Ranking substrate preference for *Pseudomonas* (specifically the model organism *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*) requires understanding its unique metabolism. Unlike *E. coli*, which prefers glucose, *Pseudomonas* exhibits **"Reverse Carbon Catabolite Repression."** It prefers organic acids and specific amino acids over sugars because they feed more directly into the Tricarboxylic Acid (TCA) cycle.

This ranking is based on growth rates, diauxic lag observations, and metabolic studies (such as those using Biolog phenotypic microarrays and Crc-mediated repression analysis).

### The Ranking (Highest to Lowest Preference)

| Rank | Substrate | Category | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Glutamate** | Amino Acid | The "gold standard" for *Pseudomonas*. Feeds directly into $\alpha$-ketoglutarate. |
| **2** | **Proline** | Amino Acid | Rapidly converted to glutamate; highly preferred in most strains. |
| **3** | **Arginine** | Amino Acid | High energy yield; *Pseudomonas* has specialized pathways for rapid arginine use. |
| **4** | **Alanine** | Amino Acid | Easily converted to pyruvate; a top-tier carbon and nitrogen source. |
| **5** | **Serine** | Amino Acid | Enters the central metabolism very efficiently via pyruvate. |
| **6** | **Mannitol** | Sugar Alcohol | Often preferred over glucose in *Pseudomonas* due to efficient conversion to fructose-6-P. |
| **7** | **Glucose** | Sugar | Used via the Entner-Doudoroff pathway, but repressed by the amino acids above. |
| **8** | **Phenylalanine** | Amino Acid | Good growth source, though catabolism is more complex than alanine. |
| **9** | **Leucine** | Amino Acid | Branched-chain amino acid; good growth but slightly slower than the "top 5." |
| **10** | **Isoleucine** | Amino Acid | Similar to leucine; utilized well but not a primary preference. |
| **11** | **Fructose** | Sugar | Utilized, but often induces a slower growth rate than glucose or mannitol. |
| **12** | **Threonine** | Amino Acid | Moderate preference; utilization is often secondary to other amino acids. |
| **13** | **Glycine** | Amino Acid | Used, but often serves more as a nitrogen source than a primary carbon source. |
| **14** | **Lysine** | Amino Acid | Catabolism is slow and complex compared to other amino acids. |
| **15** | **Adenosine** | Nucleoside | Preferred over free bases; can be used as both C and N source. |
| **16** | **4-guanidinobutanoic acid** | Organic Acid | An intermediate in arginine/agmatine decay; used but niche. |
| **17** | **Uridine** | Nucleoside | Utilized slowly; generally a secondary source. |
| **18** | **Hypoxanthine** | Purine Base | Primarily a nitrogen source; very poor carbon source. |
| **19** | **Guanine** | Purine Base | Low solubility and slow processing make it a poor carbon source. |
| **20** | **Mannose** | Sugar | *P. aeruginosa* lacks efficient pathways for mannose compared to other sugars. |
| **21** | **Arabinose** | Sugar | Most *P. aeruginosa* strains (like PAO1) cannot utilize D-arabinose. |
| **22** | **Maltose** | Disaccharide | *Pseudomonas* generally lacks the transport system for maltose. |
| **23** | **Trehalose** | Disaccharide | Typically not utilized by *P. aeruginosa*; lacks the necessary trehalase/transport. |

---

### Key Metabolic Principles for this Ranking:

1.  **The "Organic Acid/Amino Acid First" Rule:** *Pseudomonas* prioritizes substrates that enter the TCA cycle with the fewest enzymatic steps. Glutamate and Proline are almost always used before Glucose.
2.  **The Crc System:** The Carbon Catabolite Repression (CCR) in *Pseudomonas* is governed by the **Crc protein**. Crc inhibits the translation of transporters and enzymes for "lower-tier" sources (like Glucose or Mannose) when "higher-tier" sources (like Succinate or Glutamate) are present.
3.  **Sugar Limitations:** While *Pseudomonas* is metabolically versatile, it is surprisingly "sugar-poor." It lacks the Phosphotransferase System (PTS) found in enteric bacteria. It uses the **Entner-Doudoroff (ED) pathway** instead of Glycolysis (EMP pathway).
4.  **Non-Utilizable Sugars:** Many common sugars like **Maltose, Trehalose, and Arabinose** are ranked at the bottom because the standard laboratory strains (PAO1 and PA14) lack the genetic architecture to transport or cleave these molecules, effectively making them "non-carbon sources" for the species.
