Ranking substrate preference for *Pseudomonas* (specifically the model organism *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*) is unique because this genus exhibits **"Reverse Carbon Catabolite Repression" (CCR)**. Unlike *E. coli*, which prefers glucose, *Pseudomonas* prefers organic acids and specific amino acids, often repressing the consumption of glucose until the preferred sources are depleted.

The following ranking is based on growth rates, diauxic lag patterns, and metabolic hierarchy studies (e.g., the Crc/Hfq regulatory system).

### **Tier 1: Highly Preferred (Organic Acids & Primary Amino Acids)**
These substrates enter the TCA cycle directly or with minimal steps and typically repress the utilization of sugars.
1.  **Glutamate** (The most preferred; enters the TCA cycle directly via $\alpha$-ketoglutarate).
2.  **Proline** (Rapidly converted to glutamate; a top-tier carbon and nitrogen source).
3.  **Arginine** (Highly preferred; utilized via multiple pathways including the succinyltransferase pathway).
4.  **Alanine** (Easily converted to pyruvate).
5.  **Serine** (Easily converted to pyruvate).

### **Tier 2: High Preference (Secondary Amino Acids & Organic Derivatives)**
6.  **4-guanidinobutanoic acid** (An intermediate in arginine/proline metabolism; utilized efficiently).
7.  **Leucine** (Branched-chain amino acid; good growth source but slightly slower than Tier 1).
8.  **Isoleucine** (Similar to leucine).
9.  **Phenylalanine** (Aromatic amino acid; requires more complex degradation but is well-utilized).

### **Tier 3: Moderate Preference (Primary Sugars & Polyols)**
In *Pseudomonas*, these are subject to repression by the amino acids listed above.
10. **Glucose** (Utilized via the Entner-Doudoroff pathway; the "best" sugar for *Pseudomonas*).
11. **Mannitol** (A sugar alcohol utilized similarly to glucose).
12. **Lysine** (Utilized as a carbon source, but metabolism is slower than other amino acids).
13. **Threonine** (Utilized, but often less efficient than serine or alanine).

### **Tier 4: Lower Preference (Secondary Sugars & Nucleosides)**
14. **Fructose** (Enters the ED pathway but often shows a slower growth rate than glucose).
15. **Mannose** (Utilized, but less efficiently than glucose).
16. **Glycine** (A poor carbon source on its own; often used primarily as a nitrogen source).
17. **Adenosine** (Nucleoside; utilized for both carbon and nitrogen but at lower rates).
18. **Uridine** (Nucleoside; similar to adenosine).

### **Tier 5: Poorly Utilized / Non-Preferred**
Many *Pseudomonas* strains (especially *P. aeruginosa*) lack the high-affinity transporters or necessary enzymes to utilize these efficiently compared to other bacteria.
19. **Guanine** (Purine base; utilized slowly).
20. **Hypoxanthine** (Purine base; utilized slowly).
21. **Arabinose** (Many strains lack the genes for efficient pentose utilization).
22. **Trehalose** (Low preference; utilization is strain-dependent).
23. **Maltose** (Very poorly utilized by most *Pseudomonas* species; they often lack the necessary $\alpha$-glucosidases).
