Ranking substrate preference for *Pseudomonas* (specifically the model organism *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*) is complex because, unlike *E. coli*, *Pseudomonas* exhibits a "reverse" carbon catabolite repression (CCR). It prefers organic acids and certain amino acids over glucose.

This ranking is based on metabolic hierarchy studies (e.g., Palmer et al., Valentini et al., and Frimmersdorf et al.) which measure growth rates, oxygen consumption, and the order of substrate depletion in mixed cultures.

### The Hierarchy (Highest to Lowest Preference)

1.  **Glutamate** (Highly preferred; enters the TCA cycle directly via α-ketoglutarate)
2.  **Proline** (Rapidly converted to glutamate; a top-tier carbon and nitrogen source)
3.  **Arginine** (Major source of carbon/nitrogen; induces specific high-priority pathways)
4.  **Alanine** (Easily converted to pyruvate; very high preference)
5.  **Serine** (Converted to pyruvate; utilized quickly)
6.  **Glucose** (The preferred sugar, but repressed by the amino acids above)
7.  **Mannitol** (Utilized nearly as efficiently as glucose in many strains)
8.  **4-guanidinobutanoic acid** (An intermediate in arginine/agmatine catabolism; well-utilized)
9.  **Threonine** (Enters via glycine/acetyl-CoA pathways; moderately high)
10. **Leucine** (Branched-chain amino acid; good growth but slower than TCA-proximal acids)
11. **Isoleucine** (Similar to leucine)
12. **Phenylalanine** (Aromatic; requires more complex breakdown but is a solid carbon source)
13. **Adenosine** (Nucleoside; preferred over free bases as it provides ribose)
14. **Uridine** (Nucleoside; similar to adenosine)
15. **Fructose** (Utilized via the Entner-Doudoroff pathway, but often repressed by glucose)
16. **Glycine** (Small, but less efficient as a sole carbon source than larger amino acids)
17. **Lysine** (Catabolism is complex and slower compared to other amino acids)
18. **Mannose** (Can be used, but often requires induction and is less efficient than glucose)
19. **Maltose** (Utilization is variable; often requires specific transport systems not present in all strains)
20. **Trehalose** (Lower priority disaccharide)
21. **Guanine** (Purine base; used primarily as a nitrogen/nucleotide source, poor carbon source)
22. **Hypoxanthine** (Purine base; similar to guanine)
23. **Arabinose** (Lowest; most *P. aeruginosa* strains, including PAO1, lack the genes to metabolize D-arabinose efficiently)

---

### Key Factors in this Ranking:

*   **The "Organic Acid/Amino Acid First" Rule:** *Pseudomonas* evolved in environments where organic acids (like succinate, not on your list) and amino acids are abundant. It will prioritize Glutamate and Proline over Glucose.
*   **TCA Cycle Proximity:** Substrates that can be converted into TCA cycle intermediates (α-ketoglutarate, pyruvate, oxaloacetate) with the fewest enzymatic steps generally rank higher.
*   **Carbon Catabolite Repression (CCR):** In *Pseudomonas*, the Crc (Catabolite Repression Control) protein and the Hfq protein work together to block the translation of mRNAs for "lower-tier" sugars (like fructose or mannose) when "higher-tier" substrates (like glutamate) are present.
*   **Sugar Metabolism:** *Pseudomonas* lacks the Phosphofructokinase enzyme (the standard glycolysis pathway). Instead, it uses the **Entner-Doudoroff (ED) pathway**, which makes glucose metabolism slightly less energetically favorable than in enteric bacteria.
