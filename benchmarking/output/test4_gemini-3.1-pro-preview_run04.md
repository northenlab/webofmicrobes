To selectively enrich *Bacillus* species from soil while suppressing *Pedobacter*, you need to exploit the fundamental physiological and metabolic differences between the two. 

*Bacillus* species are Gram-positive, fast-growing, facultative anaerobes (mostly), and highly versatile in utilizing simple sugars and polyols. *Pedobacter* species are Gram-negative, strictly aerobic, slower-growing, and specialized in degrading complex, recalcitrant polysaccharides (like heparin, chondroitin, and chitin) but are notoriously poor at utilizing many simple carbon sources.

Here are the specific metabolites and media formulation strategies you should use to favor *Bacillus* over *Pedobacter*:

### 1. Selective Carbon Sources (Polyols and Specific Sugars)
*Pedobacter* species generally lack the transport mechanisms and enzymes to utilize sugar alcohols (polyols) and certain simple sugars, whereas *Bacillus* species thrive on them.
*   **D-Mannitol:** This is one of the best choices. Most *Bacillus* species (like *B. subtilis*, *B. megaterium*, and *B. licheniformis*) readily utilize and ferment mannitol. *Pedobacter* species almost universally test negative for mannitol utilization.
*   **Glycerol:** Similar to mannitol, glycerol is an excellent carbon source for *Bacillus* but is poorly utilized, if at all, by *Pedobacter*.
*   **Sorbitol or Inositol:** These are also generally not utilized by *Pedobacter* but can support the growth of many *Bacillus* strains.

### 2. Selective Nitrogen Sources and Electron Acceptors
*Pedobacter* is strictly aerobic and generally cannot reduce nitrate. Many *Bacillus* species are facultative anaerobes that can use nitrate as a terminal electron acceptor.
*   **Potassium Nitrate ($KNO_3$):** Use nitrate as the primary nitrogen source and electron acceptor. If you incubate your soil enrichment in **microaerophilic or anaerobic conditions** (e.g., in a sealed bottle filled to the brim, or an anaerobic jar) with nitrate, *Bacillus* will thrive via nitrate respiration, while the strictly aerobic *Pedobacter* will be completely inhibited.
*   **Inorganic Ammonium (e.g., Ammonium Sulfate):** *Bacillus* can easily synthesize all its amino acids from simple inorganic nitrogen. While some *Pedobacter* can do this, they generally prefer or require complex organic nitrogen (like peptones or yeast extract) to grow well. Keeping the media defined with inorganic nitrogen slows down *Pedobacter*.

### 3. Osmotic Modulators (Salinity)
*   **Sodium Chloride (NaCl) at 5% to 7%:** While not a carbon/nitrogen metabolite, salt is a crucial metabolic modulator. *Bacillus* species are highly halotolerant (some can grow in up to 10% NaCl). *Pedobacter* species are highly sensitive to osmotic stress and generally cannot grow at NaCl concentrations above 2% to 3%. Adding 5% NaCl to your enrichment broth will effectively eliminate *Pedobacter*.

### 4. Metabolites to AVOID (Do not add these)
To prevent *Pedobacter* from gaining a foothold, ensure your enrichment media does **not** contain:
*   **Complex polysaccharides:** Avoid chitin, pectin, xylan, heparin, or chondroitin sulfate. *Pedobacter* excels at degrading these.
*   **Complex organic nitrogen:** Avoid high concentrations of yeast extract, beef extract, or peptone. These provide the pre-formed amino acids and vitamins that allow slower-growing Gram-negatives like *Pedobacter* to survive.

### Summary: The Ideal Enrichment Broth
To achieve your goal, formulate a **defined minimal medium** containing:
1.  **D-Mannitol** or **Glycerol** (as the sole carbon source).
2.  **Ammonium sulfate** or **Potassium nitrate** (as the sole nitrogen source).
3.  **5% NaCl** (to induce osmotic stress).
4.  Standard minimal salts (phosphates, magnesium, trace minerals).

**The Ultimate Non-Metabolite Cheat Code:**
Because you are working with soil, the easiest and most foolproof way to enrich *Bacillus* and completely eradicate *Pedobacter* is **Heat Shock**. Before adding your soil to the enrichment broth, suspend the soil in sterile water and heat it to **80°C for 15 minutes**. 
*Pedobacter* does not form spores and will be entirely killed by this pasteurization. *Bacillus* forms endospores, will survive the heat shock, and will germinate rapidly once introduced to the mannitol/nitrate broth.
