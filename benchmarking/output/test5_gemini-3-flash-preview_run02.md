*Acinetobacter* species are known for their distinct metabolic preferences, often favoring organic acids and certain amino acids over sugars. When selecting carbon sources from the Northern Lab Defined Medium (NLDM) for an *Acinetobacter* isolate, it is important to align these selections with known phenotypic profiles (BPR scores) while ensuring the substrates are practical for laboratory use.

The following recommendations are based on typical BPR (Biochemical Phenotype Result) data for *Acinetobacter* isolates, such as *A. baylyi* or *A. baumannii*, grown in defined media like NLDM.

### 1. High BPR Score (Preferred Substrate): Sodium Succinate

For a high BPR score, **Sodium Succinate** is highly recommended.

*   **Rationale:** *Acinetobacter* species are highly efficient at utilizing intermediates of the Tricarboxylic Acid (TCA) cycle. Succinate consistently yields high BPR scores across most isolates as it enters central metabolism directly.
*   **Solubility:** Sodium succinate is highly soluble in water, easily exceeding the 50 mM requirement.
*   **Toxicity:** It is non-toxic at the 10–15 mM range and is a standard component in many defined growth media for this genus.
*   **Alternative:** If succinate is unavailable, **Sodium Pyruvate** is an excellent alternative with similarly high BPR scores and favorable solubility/toxicity profiles.

### 2. Neutral Score (< 5, Preferred by Most Microbes): D-Glucose

For a neutral score (typically defined as a score less than 5 but still showing some utilization) that is also a common substrate for most microbes, **D-Glucose** is the primary candidate.

*   **Rationale:** While glucose is the "universal" carbon source for many bacteria, many *Acinetobacter* strains lack a complete glycolytic pathway (specifically the hexokinase or glucokinase enzymes) or prefer the Entner-Doudoroff pathway. This often results in moderate or "neutral" BPR scores compared to their preferred organic acids.
*   **Solubility:** D-Glucose is highly soluble in water (>50 mM).
*   **Toxicity:** It is non-toxic at 10–15 mM.
*   **Alternative:** **L-Alanine** often yields neutral to moderate scores in *Acinetobacter* and is widely utilized by other microbes.

### 3. Low BPR Score (Control): D-Sorbitol

For the lowest BPR score to serve as a negative control, **D-Sorbitol** is recommended.

*   **Rationale:** Most *Acinetobacter* isolates lack the metabolic machinery to utilize sugar alcohols like sorbitol. In phenotypic assays, this substrate typically yields a score of zero or near-zero, making it an ideal negative control for growth experiments.
*   **Solubility:** D-Sorbitol is highly soluble in water (>50 mM).
*   **Toxicity:** It is non-toxic at 10–15 mM.
*   **Alternative:** If D-Sorbitol shows unexpected utilization, **Sucrose** is another common NLDM component that many *Acinetobacter* strains cannot metabolize, typically resulting in a very low BPR score.
