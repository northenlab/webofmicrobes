Ranking the substrate preference for *Pseudomonas* (specifically the model organism *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*) is based on the phenomenon of **Reverse Carbon Catabolite Repression (CCR)**. Unlike *E. coli*, which prefers glucose, *Pseudomonas* prefers organic acids and specific amino acids that enter the TCA cycle directly or with minimal steps.

The following ranking is based on growth rates and the hierarchical preference observed in metabolic studies (e.g., Palmer et al., 2007; Rojo, 2010).

### **Tier 1: Highly Preferred (TCA Cycle Intermediates & Direct Precursors)**
These substrates are utilized first and will often repress the uptake of glucose and other sugars.
1.  **Glutamate** (Enters directly as $\alpha$-ketoglutarate; the "gold standard" for *Pseudomonas*)
2.  **Proline** (Easily converted to glutamate)
3.  **Alanine** (Directly converted to pyruvate)
4.  **Arginine** (Major carbon and nitrogen source; highly efficient)
5.  **4-guanidinobutanoic acid** (An intermediate in the arginine/guanidino pathway; highly preferred)
6.  **Serine** (Converted directly to pyruvate)

### **Tier 2: Preferred Sugars and Polyols**
*Pseudomonas* uses the Entner-Doudoroff (ED) pathway rather than the Glycolytic pathway.
7.  **Glucose** (The most preferred sugar, but repressed by Tier 1)
8.  **Mannitol** (Polyol that enters the ED pathway efficiently)
9.  **Fructose** (Enters via the PTS system or specialized pathways)
10. **Mannose** (Utilized well, but generally slower than glucose)

### **Tier 3: Nucleosides and Secondary Amino Acids**
11. **Adenosine** (Nucleosides are generally preferred over free bases)
12. **Uridine**
13. **Leucine** (Branched-chain amino acid; requires more steps to enter TCA)
14. **Isoleucine** (Branched-chain amino acid)
15. **Threonine**
16. **Phenylalanine** (Aromatic amino acid; complex degradation pathway)
17. **Lysine** (Slowly metabolized compared to other amino acids)

### **Tier 4: Poorly Utilized or Specialized Substrates**
These substrates often support very slow growth or are only used if no other sources are available.
18. **Trehalose** (Disaccharide; *Pseudomonas* lacks high-efficiency uptake for this compared to *E. coli*)
19. **Maltose** (Often poorly utilized by many *Pseudomonas* strains)
20. **Arabinose** (Many *P. aeruginosa* strains lack the full operon for L-arabinose utilization)
21. **Hypoxanthine** (Purine base; primarily used as a nitrogen source, poor carbon source)
22. **Guanine** (Purine base; very low solubility and slow metabolism)
23. **Glycine** (While a nitrogen source, it is an extremely poor carbon source for growth in *Pseudomonas*)
