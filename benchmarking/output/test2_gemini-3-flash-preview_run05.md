To predict which bacterial genera are most competitive for the 20 proteogenic amino acids, the following three sequential steps were performed.

### Step 1: Predict Doubling Time and Filter
The doubling time (DT) for each genus was predicted based on representative species under optimal laboratory conditions. Genera with a predicted doubling time greater than 5 hours were removed.

*   **Genera Removed (DT > 5h):** *Afipia, Bradyrhizobium, Chitinophaga, Curvibacter, Lysobacter, Marmoricola, Mesorhizobium, Methylobacterium, Modestobacter, Mucilaginibacter, Niastella, Novosphingobium, Pedobacter, Phenylobacterium, Sphingomonas.*
*   **Genera Remaining (DT ≤ 5h):** *Acidovorax, Acinetobacter, Arthrobacter, Bacillus, Brevibacillus, Brevundimonas, Burkholderia, Caulobacter, Chryseobacterium, Cupriavidus, Ensifer, Flavobacterium, Herbaspirillum, Janthinobacterium, Lysinibacillus, Massilia, Paenibacillus, Pseudomonas, Rhizobium, Rhodococcus, Stenotrophomonas, Streptomyces, Undibacterium, Zoogloea.*

### Step 2: Calculate Average Preference for Proteogenic Amino Acids
For the remaining 24 genera, the average substrate preference for the 20 proteogenic amino acids was calculated on a scale of 0.0 to 1.0. This calculation considers the density of ABC transporters, the presence of specific permeases, and the metabolic priority of amino acids relative to other carbon sources (e.g., sugars).
