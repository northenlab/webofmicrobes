Ranking substrate preference for *Pseudomonas* (specifically the model organism *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*) is unique because these bacteria exhibit **"Reverse Catabolite Repression."** Unlike *E. coli*, which prefers glucose above all else, *Pseudomonas* prefers organic acids and specific amino acids, often repressing the consumption of glucose until the preferred sources are depleted.

This ranking is based on growth rates, carbon catabolite repression (CCR) hierarchy studies (the Crc/Hfq system), and metabolic flux analysis.

### The Ranking (Highest to Lowest Preference)

1.  **Glutamate** (The gold standard; enters the TCA cycle directly via α-ketoglutarate)
2.  **Proline** (Highly preferred; rapidly converted to glutamate)
3.  **Alanine** (Easily converted to pyruvate; high preference)
4.  **Arginine** (Excellent carbon and nitrogen source; high hierarchy)
5.  **Serine** (Directly converted to pyruvate)
6.  **Glucose** (The primary sugar, but repressed by the amino acids above)
7.  **Mannitol** (Utilized almost as efficiently as glucose in many strains)
8.  **Threonine**
9.  **Leucine** (Branched-chain amino acids are good sources but require more steps)
10. **Isoleucine**
11. **Phenylalanine**
12. **Lysine**
13. **Glycine** (Often a poor carbon source on its own; used more for nitrogen)
14. **Fructose** (Metabolized via the ED pathway, but often repressed by glucose/AAs)
15. **Mannose**
16. **4-guanidinobutanoic acid** (An intermediate in arginine catabolism; used but lower in hierarchy)
17. **Adenosine** (Nucleoside; used primarily as a nitrogen/salvage source)
18. **Uridine** (Nucleoside)
19. **Hypoxanthine** (Purine base; low energy yield as a carbon source)
20. **Guanine** (Purine base)
21. **Trehalose** (Low preference; many *P. aeruginosa* strains lack efficient uptake)
22. **Maltose** (Very low preference; often not utilized by *P. aeruginosa* PAO1)
23. **Arabinose** (Generally not utilized by *P. aeruginosa*; more common in *P. putida*)

---

### Key Factors Determining this Hierarchy:

#### 1. The "Organic Acid/Amino Acid First" Rule
*Pseudomonas* evolved to live in environments like the rhizosphere or animal tissues where organic acids (like succinate, not on your list) and amino acids are abundant. **Glutamate, Proline, and Alanine** are almost always consumed first because they provide both carbon and nitrogen with very few enzymatic steps required to enter the TCA cycle.

#### 2. Reverse Catabolite Repression (CCR)
In *E. coli*, glucose inhibits the use of lactose. In *Pseudomonas*, the **Crc (Catabolite Repression Control)** protein and the small RNA **crcZ** regulate metabolism so that amino acids inhibit the use of **Glucose**. If you provide a mix of Glutamate and Glucose, the *Pseudomonas* will ignore the Glucose until the Glutamate is gone.

#### 3. Sugar Metabolism (The ED Pathway)
*Pseudomonas* lacks the phosphofructokinase enzyme, meaning it cannot perform classical Glycolysis (EMP pathway). Instead, it uses the **Entner-Doudoroff (ED) pathway**. This makes sugars like **Glucose and Mannitol** slightly less energetically favorable than amino acids that enter the TCA cycle more directly.

#### 4. Specialized/Poor Substrates
*   **Nucleosides/Bases:** While *Pseudomonas* can use adenosine or guanine, they are primarily used to salvage nucleotides for DNA/RNA synthesis rather than as bulk carbon sources for energy.
*   **Disaccharides (Maltose/Trehalose):** Many *Pseudomonas* species are specialized for "simple" monomers. Complex sugars like maltose often rank at the very bottom because the bacteria may lack the specific porins (like LamB in *E. coli*) or hydrolases to process them efficiently.
