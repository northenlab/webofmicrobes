Selectively enriching *Streptomyces* while suppressing *Pseudomonas* is a classic challenge in soil microbiology. *Pseudomonas* species are "r-strategists"—they grow rapidly on simple sugars and organic acids. *Streptomyces* are "K-strategists"—they grow more slowly but possess a massive enzymatic toolkit for degrading complex, recalcitrant polymers.

To favor *Streptomyces*, you must move away from "easy" carbon sources and utilize substrates that require specialized extracellular enzymes.

### 1. Chitin and N-acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc)
This is the most effective metabolic strategy. *Streptomyces* are among the primary chitin degraders in soil.
*   **Why it works:** *Streptomyces* possess multiple chitinase genes and a highly specialized uptake system for GlcNAc. While some *Pseudomonas* can utilize GlcNAc, it acts as a **morphogenetic signal** for *Streptomyces*, triggering both aerial mycelium formation and antibiotic production.
*   **Application:** Supplement soil with colloidal chitin or high concentrations of GlcNAc. In *Streptomyces*, GlcNAc accumulation leads to the repression of primary metabolism genes for other carbons, effectively "locking" them into a specialized growth mode that many competitors cannot match.

### 2. Starch (Complex α-glucans)
While many bacteria can use glucose, *Streptomyces* are prolific producers of extracellular amylases.
*   **Why it works:** Many soil *Pseudomonas* species (like *P. putida*) are actually amylase-negative or very weak starch degraders. They prefer organic acids (citrate, succinate) or glucose. By providing starch as the sole carbon source, you force the community to rely on extracellular hydrolysis, a niche dominated by Actinomycetes.
*   **Application:** Use soluble starch as the primary carbon source in a minimal salts medium (e.g., Gauze’s Medium No. 1).

### 3. L-Arabinose
Metabolic profiling (Biolog plates) often shows a divergence in pentose utilization.
*   **Why it works:** Many *Streptomyces* species efficiently utilize L-arabinose. While some *Pseudomonas* can use it, they generally prioritize organic acids or glucose via the Entner-Doudoroff pathway. In a competitive environment, *Streptomyces* often outcompete Gram-negatives for specific pentoses found in hemicellulose.

### 4. Myo-Inositol
*   **Why it works:** Utilization of inositol is a classic taxonomic marker for identifying *Streptomyces* species. While *Pseudomonas* metabolic diversity is vast, many common soil fluorescent pseudomonads lack the specific cyclase and dehydrogenase pathways to efficiently utilize inositol as a primary carbon source compared to the robust pathways in Actinomycetes.

### 5. Nitrate as the Sole Nitrogen Source (with high C:N ratio)
*   **Why it works:** *Pseudomonas* are highly efficient at using amino acids and ammonium. However, *Streptomyces* are very well-adapted to nitrogen-limited environments and can efficiently scavenge nitrate. By providing a complex carbon source (like cellulose or starch) and nitrate, you favor the slow-and-steady filamentous growth of *Streptomyces*.

### Strategic Implementation (The "Selective Pressure" Combo)

To achieve the best results, do not rely on metabolites alone. Use a combination of chemical and physical pressures:

1.  **The "Dry-Down" Pre-treatment:** *Streptomyces* produce desiccation-resistant spores; *Pseudomonas* are highly sensitive to drying. Air-dry your soil sample for 48–72 hours before inoculation/enrichment. This significantly reduces the viable *Pseudomonas* population.
2.  **Alkaline pH:** Adjust your enrichment medium to **pH 8.0–8.5**. *Streptomyces* are generally more tolerant of alkaline conditions, whereas many soil *Pseudomonas* prefer neutral to slightly acidic (pH 6.0–7.0) environments.
3.  **Calcium Carbonate ($CaCO_3$):** Mix the soil with $CaCO_3$ (10% w/w) and incubate in a moist chamber before isolation. This creates micro-environments that favor Actinomycete spore germination over Proteobacteria.
4.  **Antibiotic Selection (Optional):** If you are using a culture medium, add **Nalidixic acid** (approx. 20–50 µg/ml). Nalidixic acid inhibits the DNA gyrase of many Gram-negative bacteria like *Pseudomonas* but is much less effective against *Streptomyces*.

### Summary Recommendation
Use a **Minimal Salts Medium** supplemented with **0.5% Colloidal Chitin** or **Starch**, adjusted to **pH 8.2**, and ensure the soil has been **pre-dried**. This combination creates a metabolic and environmental "bottleneck" that *Pseudomonas* struggle to pass, but *Streptomyces* thrive in.
