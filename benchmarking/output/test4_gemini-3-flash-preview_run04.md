Selectively enriching *Streptomyces* while suppressing *Pseudomonas* is a classic challenge in soil microbiology. *Pseudomonas* species are "r-strategists"—they grow rapidly on simple sugars and organic acids, often outcompeting the slower-growing, filamentous *Streptomyces*.

To favor *Streptomyces*, you must pivot away from "easy" carbon sources and toward complex polymers and specific nutrient ratios that favor the secondary metabolism and filamentous morphology of Actinomycetes.

Here are the best metabolites and strategies for selective enrichment:

### 1. Chitin (The Gold Standard)
Chitin (a polymer of N-acetylglucosamine) is the most effective substrate for enriching *Streptomyces*.
*   **Why it works:** *Streptomyces* are among the primary degraders of chitin in soil, possessing a robust suite of extracellular chitinases. While some *Pseudomonas* species can degrade chitin, they are generally far less efficient at it than *Streptomyces*.
*   **Application:** Use **colloidal chitin** as the primary carbon source. In agar or soil microcosms, this forces *Pseudomonas* to rely on the slow release of monomers, while *Streptomyces* can colonize the chitin particles directly with their hyphae.

### 2. Starch (High Molecular Weight)
*Streptomyces* are prolific producers of amylases, allowing them to break down complex polysaccharides.
*   **Why it works:** While many bacteria use starch, *Pseudomonas* generally prefers organic acids (like citrate or succinate) or simple sugars. By using starch as the sole carbon source, you slow down the "explosive" growth phase of *Pseudomonas*.
*   **Note:** This is the basis for **Starch Casein Agar (SCA)**, a standard medium for isolating *Streptomyces*.

### 3. Glycerol + Asparagine
This combination is the basis for International Streptomyces Project (ISP) Medium 5.
*   **Why it works:** *Streptomyces* utilize glycerol very efficiently as a carbon source. While *Pseudomonas* can use glycerol, it does not support the same rapid "swarming" growth that glucose or organic acids do. Asparagine serves as a selective nitrogen source that favors the development of aerial mycelium and sporulation in *Streptomyces*.

### 4. Arginine
Using Arginine as a combined Carbon and Nitrogen source can be selective.
*   **Why it works:** Many *Streptomyces* can utilize arginine via the arginine deiminase pathway to survive in nutrient-poor conditions. While *Pseudomonas* also uses arginine, *Streptomyces* are often more competitive on **Arginine-Glycerol-Salts (AGS)** media, which is designed to limit the growth of fast-growing Gram-negative bacteria.

### 5. Humic Acids
*   **Why it works:** Humic acids are complex, recalcitrant carbon sources. *Streptomyces* (and other Actinomycetes) are specialized in breaking down these "tough" organic matters. *Pseudomonas* typically lacks the enzymatic machinery to derive significant energy from humic substances.
*   **Application:** Humic Acid-Vitamin (HV) agar is a specialized medium used to isolate rare Actinomycetes from soil.

### Strategies to Enhance Selectivity
Metabolites alone may not be enough because *Pseudomonas* is metabolically "promiscuous." You should combine the metabolites above with the following environmental pressures:

#### A. pH Manipulation (Alkalinity)
*   *Streptomyces* generally prefer slightly alkaline conditions (**pH 8.0 to 8.5**).
*   *Pseudomonas* thrives at neutral pH (7.0). Raising the soil or media pH slightly can significantly inhibit many *Pseudomonas* strains while favoring *Streptomyces*.

#### B. Water Potential (Desiccation)
*   *Streptomyces* produce desiccation-resistant spores.
*   If you are working with soil samples, **air-drying the soil** for 2–4 days at room temperature before enrichment will kill a large percentage of vegetative *Pseudomonas* cells while leaving *Streptomyces* spores viable.

#### C. Chemical Inhibition (The "Nuclear" Option)
If you are using a broth or agar for enrichment, add specific antibiotics that *Streptomyces* are naturally resistant to, but *Pseudomonas* is sensitive to:
*   **Nalidixic Acid:** Highly effective against Gram-negative bacteria like *Pseudomonas* by inhibiting DNA gyrase.
*   **Cycloheximide:** To prevent fungal overgrowth, which often occurs when using chitin or starch.

### Summary Recommendation
For the best results, use a **Colloidal Chitin-based medium** adjusted to **pH 8.0**, and pre-treat your soil by **air-drying** it. This combination targets the structural niche (chitinase production), the physiological niche (alkalinity), and the survival niche (spore formation) of *Streptomyces*.
