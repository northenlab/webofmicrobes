To selectively enrich *Streptomyces* while suppressing *Pseudomonas*, you must exploit the fundamental ecological differences between them. *Pseudomonas* species are "r-strategists"—they grow rapidly on simple, easily accessible sugars and organic acids. *Streptomyces* are "K-strategists"—they grow slowly but possess a massive repertoire of extracellular enzymes to break down complex, recalcitrant polymers.

The most effective approach is to provide **complex polysaccharides** that require specialized degradation machinery which *Pseudomonas* generally lacks or finds energetically unfavorable.

Here are the best metabolites and substrates for this purpose:

### 1. Chitin (The Gold Standard)
Chitin is a polymer of N-acetylglucosamine found in fungal cell walls and arthropod exoskeletons.
*   **Why it works:** *Streptomyces* are among the most prolific chitin degraders in soil. They possess multiple chitinase genes and specialized uptake systems. While some *Pseudomonas* species can degrade chitin, they are rarely as efficient as actinomycetes in a competitive soil environment.
*   **Application:** Amend soil with **chitin flakes** or **colloidal chitin**. This triggers a "chitinoclastic" response, where *Streptomyces* germinate from spores to colonize the chitin particles.

### 2. Soluble Starch
Starch is a classic carbon source used in selective media for *Streptomyces* (e.g., Inorganic Salts-Starch Agar or ISP4).
*   **Why it works:** Most *Streptomyces* produce potent extracellular $\alpha$-amylases. Conversely, many common soil *Pseudomonas* (such as *P. fluorescens* and *P. putida*) are amylase-negative or possess very weak amylolytic activity. They prefer to wait for other organisms to break starch down into glucose, but by then, the *Streptomyces* have often established dominance.
*   **Application:** Use soluble starch as the primary carbon source in a low-nutrient background.

### 3. Myo-Inositol
Inositol is a sugar alcohol that is a common component of plant phytates.
*   **Why it works:** Utilization of *myo*-inositol is a diagnostic metabolic trait for many *Streptomyces* species (used in the International Streptomyces Project). While some *Pseudomonas* can utilize it, many cannot, or they prioritize other carbon sources (like citrate or succinate) if present.
*   **Application:** Use as a supplemental carbon source in a defined minimal medium.

### 4. Raffinose
Raffinose is a trisaccharide (galactose, glucose, and fructose) found in many plants.
*   **Why it works:** *Streptomyces* frequently possess the $\alpha$-galactosidases required to cleave raffinose. Many *Pseudomonas* species lack the ability to utilize raffinose as a sole carbon source, making it a common ingredient in selective actinomycete isolation media.

### 5. Xylan (Hemicellulose)
Xylan is a complex polysaccharide found in plant cell walls.
*   **Why it works:** *Streptomyces* are major contributors to the degradation of lignocellulosic biomass. They secrete xylanases to break the polymer into xylose. *Pseudomonas* are generally poor at degrading complex hemicellulose, preferring the monomeric sugars once they are already released.

---

### Strategies to Further Discourage *Pseudomonas*
Metabolites alone may not be enough if the environment favors fast-growing Gram-negatives. Combine the substrates above with these environmental pressures:

1.  **Nitrogen Source (Arginine):** Use **L-arginine** as the sole nitrogen source. *Streptomyces* utilize arginine efficiently via the urea cycle, whereas many other bacteria find it a difficult sole nitrogen source.
2.  **Water Stress (Desiccation):** *Pseudomonas* are highly sensitive to low water potential. *Streptomyces* produce desiccation-resistant spores. Allowing the soil to undergo drying cycles will naturally favor the sporulating *Streptomyces*.
3.  **pH Adjustment:** Aim for a slightly alkaline pH (**7.5 to 8.5**). *Streptomyces* tolerate alkaline conditions well, whereas many soil *Pseudomonas* prefer neutral to slightly acidic conditions.
4.  **Avoid "Pseudomonas Bait":** Strictly avoid **Glucose, Citrate, Succinate, and Malate**. These are the "fast foods" of the microbial world; *Pseudomonas* will use them to outgrow *Streptomyces* within hours.

### Summary Recommendation
To enrich *Streptomyces* specifically, use a **Chitin-Starch-Arginine** based amendment. This forces the microbial community to rely on complex polymer degradation (Chitin/Starch) and a complex nitrogen metabolism (Arginine), niches where *Streptomyces* excel and *Pseudomonas* struggle.
