Ranking the metabolic preferences of *Pseudomonas* requires understanding a fundamental quirk of its biology: **Carbon Catabolite Repression (CCR) in *Pseudomonas* is the exact opposite of *E. coli*.** 

While *E. coli* prefers sugars (like glucose) over everything else, *Pseudomonas* species (modeled heavily by *P. aeruginosa* and *P. putida*) strongly prefer **amino acids and organic acids over sugars**. When preferred amino acids are present, the bacteria will actively repress the genes needed to consume glucose and other carbohydrates.

Based on the biochemical pathways, transport kinetics, and CCR hierarchy of the *Pseudomonas* genus, here is the ranking of the 23 substrates from highest to lowest preference.

### **Tier 1: The Absolute Preferences (The Glutamate Family & Pyruvate Formers)**
These substrates are consumed first. They yield high energy, feed directly into the TCA cycle, and actively repress the consumption of sugars and other amino acids.

**1. Glutamate** *(The undisputed #1 preferred carbon and nitrogen source for Pseudomonas)*
**2. Proline** *(Rapidly converted directly into glutamate via the PutA enzyme)*
**3. Arginine** *(Highly preferred; feeds directly into glutamate and the TCA cycle)*
**4. Alanine** *(Easily and rapidly converted directly into pyruvate)*
**5. Serine** *(Easily converted to pyruvate)*

### **Tier 2: Secondary Amino Acids & Preferred Sugars**
Once the top-tier amino acids are depleted, *Pseudomonas* will turn to its preferred sugars and branched-chain/aromatic amino acids. *Pseudomonas* lacks the EMP (glycolysis) pathway and must metabolize sugars via the Entner-Doudoroff (ED) pathway.

**6. Glucose** *(The most preferred sugar, but its consumption is repressed if substrates 1-5 are present)*
**7. Fructose** *(Metabolized very efficiently alongside or just after glucose)*
**8. Mannitol** *(A sugar alcohol that feeds directly into the fructose metabolic pathway)*
**9. Leucine** *(Branched-chain amino acid; good carbon/energy source)*
**10. Isoleucine** *(Branched-chain amino acid; metabolized similarly to leucine)*
**11. Phenylalanine** *(Pseudomonas species are famous for their robust aromatic degradation pathways, making this a strong carbon source, though it requires more enzymatic steps than simple amino acids)*

### **Tier 3: Tertiary Amino Acids & Specific Intermediates**
These are viable carbon and nitrogen sources but are generally consumed slower or require specific induction compared to the higher tiers.

**12. 4-guanidinobutanoic acid** *(An intermediate in the arginine degradation pathway; readily utilized because the arginine machinery is highly active in Pseudomonas)*
**13. Lysine** *(Metabolized via the AMV or AVA pathways; a good source but slower than the glutamate family)*
**14. Threonine** *(Can be utilized, but often secondary to other amino acids)*
**15. Glycine** *(Used more for C1 metabolism and purine biosynthesis rather than as a bulk primary carbon source)*

### **Tier 4: Nucleosides and Nucleobases**
*Pseudomonas* can salvage and catabolize these compounds, primarily to extract nitrogen. However, they are poor primary carbon sources compared to amino acids and simple carbohydrates.

**16. Adenosine** *(Nucleoside; yields a ribose sugar and a base)*
**17. Uridine** *(Nucleoside)*
**18. Hypoxanthine** *(Purine derivative)*
**19. Guanine** *(Purine base; requires significant energy to break the ring structure for carbon)*

### **Tier 5: Poorly Utilized or Non-Utilized Sugars**
*Pseudomonas* is notoriously "picky" with carbohydrates. It lacks the transporters and enzymatic machinery to utilize many complex sugars. 

**20. Mannose** *(Utilization is highly strain-dependent; some can use it slowly, many cannot)*
**21. Trehalose** *(Synthesized by Pseudomonas as an osmoprotectant during stress, but generally a poor primary carbon source for bulk growth)*
**22. Arabinose** *(Most clinical and environmental strains of P. aeruginosa cannot utilize this pentose sugar; it is often used as a negative control in biochemical identification)*
**23. Maltose** *(Pseudomonas generally lacks the transport machinery and maltase enzymes to utilize this disaccharide; it will not grow if this is the sole carbon source).*

***

### **Summary of the *Pseudomonas* Metabolic Logic:**
1. **Glutamate/Proline/Arginine** are the kings of the *Pseudomonas* diet.
2. **Glucose/Fructose** are the only sugars it truly "likes," but it will ignore them if amino acids are present.
3. **Nucleobases** are scavenged for nitrogen, not preferred for carbon.
4. **Complex/Alternative sugars** (Maltose, Arabinose) are essentially useless to them.
