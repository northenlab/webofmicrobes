Ranking substrate preference for *Pseudomonas* (specifically the model organism *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*) is distinct from other bacteria like *E. coli*. *Pseudomonas* exhibits **Carbon Catabolite Repression (CCR)** that favors organic acids and specific amino acids over glucose and other sugars.

This ranking is based on growth rate, uptake priority in competition assays (diauxic growth patterns), and the efficiency of the Entner-Doudoroff (ED) pathway versus the TCA cycle.

### **Tier 1: The Highly Preferred (TCA Cycle Intermediates & Related Amino Acids)**
These substrates are utilized first and will actively repress the metabolism of glucose and other sugars.
1.  **Glutamate:** Often the #1 preferred carbon and nitrogen source; enters the TCA cycle directly via $\alpha$-ketoglutarate.
2.  **Proline:** Highly preferred; *Pseudomonas* has specialized high-affinity transporters (PutP) for it.
3.  **Arginine:** A major source of carbon, nitrogen, and energy; can be metabolized aerobically and anaerobically.
4.  **Alanine:** Easily converted to pyruvate; utilized very early in growth phases.
5.  **Serine:** Directly converted to pyruvate; highly efficient for *Pseudomonas* metabolism.

### **Tier 2: Primary Sugars and Specialized Amino Acids**
*Pseudomonas* prefers these once the Tier 1 amino acids are depleted.
6.  **Glucose:** Unlike *E. coli*, glucose is a secondary choice compared to organic acids/certain amino acids, but it is the most preferred sugar.
7.  **Mannitol:** A sugar alcohol that *Pseudomonas* utilizes very efficiently via the ED pathway.
8.  **Fructose:** Utilized well, though often slightly behind glucose in transport priority.
9.  **4-guanidinobutanoic acid:** An intermediate in the arginine/agmatine catabolic pathway; *Pseudomonas* is specialized in utilizing guanidino compounds.
10. **Phenylalanine:** *Pseudomonas* species are metabolic specialists in aromatic compounds; phenylalanine is funneled into the homogentisate pathway.

### **Tier 3: Nucleosides and Branched-Chain Amino Acids**
11. **Adenosine:** Nucleosides are generally preferred over free bases as they provide both a ribose sugar and a nitrogenous base.
12. **Uridine:** Utilized similarly to adenosine for carbon and nitrogen.
13. **Leucine:** Branched-chain amino acid; metabolized to Acetyl-CoA and Acetoacetate.
14. **Isoleucine:** Metabolized to Acetyl-CoA and Succinyl-CoA.
15. **Threonine:** Can be converted to pyruvate or used in specialized pathways, but growth is slower than on Tier 1 acids.

### **Tier 4: Poorly Utilized or Complex Substrates**
These substrates often support slow growth or are only used when no other options are available.
16. **Glycine:** A very small molecule, but *Pseudomonas* often struggles to use it as a sole carbon source efficiently.
17. **Lysine:** Catabolism is complex (the cadaverine or aminoadipate pathways) and is generally slow.
18. **Mannose:** While many bacteria love mannose, *P. aeruginosa* often lacks efficient mannose-specific transport compared to glucose.
19. **Hypoxanthine:** A free purine base; requires significant processing compared to nucleosides.
20. **Guanine:** Similar to hypoxanthine; used primarily as a nitrogen source rather than a primary carbon source.

### **Tier 5: The "Non-Preferred" (Often Non-utilizable)**
*P. aeruginosa* lacks the specific transport systems (like the PTS system in enterics) for many complex or alternative sugars.
21. **Arabinose:** Most *P. aeruginosa* strains cannot utilize D-arabinose as a carbon source.
22. **Trehalose:** Generally not utilized by *P. aeruginosa* (though some other *Pseudomonas* species can).
23. **Maltose:** *P. aeruginosa* notoriously lacks the classical maltose transport and degradation system found in *E. coli*.
