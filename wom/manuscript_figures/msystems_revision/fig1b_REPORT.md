## Summary

- **The reviewer is right on both points.** The submitted Fig. 1b compares each withheld consumed compound against every
  non-training-positive compound (neutral + secreted + untested). Its "Ordinal-BPR" curve was trained with an **empty**
  known-negative set, so the ordinal modification was inert and the curve shows the plain BPR objective.
- **The submitted numbers reproduce.** Re-running the unmodified code gives Ordinal-BPR AUC 0.944 ± 0.004 at dim 32 (submitted: 0.944 ± 0.006).
  WR-MF, SVD and LMF match the submitted CSV to within 5 × 10⁻⁷.
- **With both bugs fixed, Ordinal-BPR does not beat plain BPR at ranking consumed above measured-neutral compounds.**
  - On the leave-one-out neutral pool (A1), it loses in 29 of 30 paired runs (dim 32: 0.792 vs 0.856).
  - On the strict hold-out (B), it ties BPR at dims 4–32 (dim 32: 0.827 vs 0.824) and is worse at 64–128.
  - It is clearly better than BPR only on pooled PR-AUC: +0.09 to +0.13 at every dimension, 30/30 runs.
- **The "stable across dimensions" result belonged to the BPR objective, not to the ordinal modification.**
  - After the fixes, plain BPR is the most stable model on A1 and on B per-microbe ROC-AUC.
  - Corrected Ordinal-BPR degrades steeply above 32 dimensions on both.
- **Within Ordinal-BPR, the hard negatives help and the ordinal term hurts** (dim 32, budget-matched ablation).
  - With `lambda_ord = 0`: A1 rises from 0.792 to **0.895** and B from 0.827 to **0.865**. Both are the best of any model tested at dim 32.
  - With the hard negatives also removed (`hard_neg_ratio = 0`): A1 falls to 0.520.
- **The deployed model and Fig. S3 are genuine Ordinal-BPR.** Both are trained through `prepare_training_data` → `train_model`,
  which injects the known negatives (`trained_models/ordinal_bpr_consumed/metadata.json` contains them). Only the Fig. 1b
  benchmark dropped them.

## 2. Bug confirmation

### 2a. Known negatives are dropped, so "Ordinal-BPR" trained as plain BPR

- **The data are dropped when the split is built.** `make_figures_for_manuscript_nature_microbiology_first_submission.py:256-264` rebuilds the per-repeat
  `TrainingData` without a `known_negatives` argument, so it takes the dataclass default `{}` (`core/types.py:105`).
- **Nothing restores them at training time.** `train_model()` injects `training_data.known_negatives` (`core/training.py:48`), which is that empty dict.
- **The ordinal machinery therefore never runs.** Inside `OrdinalBPRModel.fit()` (`core/models.py:467`), `known_negatives or {}` stays empty, so `has_hard` is always False.
  - No hard negative is ever drawn (line 489).
  - No ordinal pair is ever formed (line 494; ordinal pairs are built only in the hard-negative branch).
  - Soft negatives are drawn from all non-positives (line 504), exactly as in plain BPR.

Evidence (`fig1b_bug_checks.json`):

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| `known_negatives` reaching `OrdinalBPRModel.fit()` in the **unmodified** submitted benchmark (all 30 Ordinal-BPR fits, recorded by wrapping `fit`) | **0** in every call. The full training data has 59,749 |
| Submitted split, dim 32, same seed: default Ordinal-BPR vs `hard_neg_ratio=0, lambda_ord=0` (modification switched off) | **Bit-identical embeddings**; identical AUC (0.9365) |
| Same split and seed with known negatives passed through | Different embeddings; mixed-pool AUC 0.847 |

The submitted "Ordinal-BPR" is not bit-identical to the `BPRModel` class. `BPRModel` runs `len(microbes)//256 + 1` = 2 minibatches
per epoch (`core/models.py:155`), while `OrdinalBPRModel` runs `max(1, len//256)` = 1 (line 478). It is the plain BPR loss and
sampler with half the gradient steps.

### 2b. The negative pool is neutral + secreted + untested

`compute_loo_auc()` (`make_figures_for_manuscript_nature_microbiology_first_submission.py:203-207`) keeps every item with
`i != test_item and i not in exclude_from_ranking`, where `exclude_from_ranking` is the microbe's training positives (lines 280–284).

For the 427 evaluated microbes, this "mixed" pool contains 121,707 negatives:
- **58,225 measured-neutral**
- **59,583 never measured**
- **3,899 secreted**

The Results text ("…higher predicted rank than a randomly selected neutral substrate") therefore does not describe the computation.

Our Protocol A3 "mixed" pool equals `compute_loo_auc()` exactly on the deterministic models (difference 0). This confirms the definition.

## 4. Corrected benchmark

**Labels** come from the per-row assertion: consumed ≤ −2, neutral −2 < v < +2, secreted ≥ +2.
- A microbe–compound pair is *consumed* if any row is consumed. These are exactly the training positives.
- It is *secreted* if any row is secreted and none is consumed.
- It is *neutral* if all rows are neutral, and *untested* if it was never measured.
- Totals: 11,093 consumed, 59,573 neutral, 3,938 secreted and 60,059 untested pairs.
- **Secreted pairs are never used as negatives.**

**Training settings** match the submitted run (Ordinal-BPR: 1000 epochs, batch 256, lr 0.01, `hard_neg_ratio` 0.5, `lambda_ord` 0.1;
WR-MF: 50 iterations, reg 0.1; SVD; LMF: 30 iterations, lr 1.0, reg 0.1). Plain BPR uses 1000 epochs, batch 256, lr 0.01, as in the 6-model
benchmark of `make_figures_for_manuscript_ordinal.py`. Before each fit, `random`, `numpy` and `torch` are seeded with
10,000 × repeat + dim. All models at a given repeat and dimension share a seed and a split, so comparisons are paired.

**Protocol A, leave-one-out** (the submitted split, `leave_one_out_split(seed=repeat)`):
- One consumed compound is withheld for each of the 427 microbes with ≥ 2 consumed compounds.
- Ordinal-BPR receives the full 59,749 known negatives.
- Per-pair AUC is averaged over microbes, against three pools: A1 the microbe's neutral compounds, A2 its never-measured compounds, A3 all non-training-positives.
- All 427 microbes have a non-empty pool in all three cases. The negative counts are 58,225 (A1), 59,583 (A2) and 121,707 (A3).

**Protocol B, strict hold-out:**
- For each repeat and microbe, 20% of consumed pairs and 20% of neutral pairs are withheld (rounded half up, at least 1).
- A class is withheld only if the microbe has ≥ 2 pairs of that class, so at least one stays in training. Three microbes with a single consumed pair contribute withheld neutrals only.
- Withheld pairs are removed from the positives and from Ordinal-BPR's known negatives (47,835 remain), so they are untested for every model.
- Each repeat withholds 2,228 consumed + 11,914 neutral = 14,142 pairs (prevalence 0.158). All 427 microbes with ≥ 2 consumed pairs have both classes withheld.

## 7. Why the ordinal term hurts in leave-one-out

**Tier means.** Mean Protocol A scores at dim 32 for the withheld consumed compound, the microbe's neutral compounds and its untested compounds:

| Model | withheld consumed | neutral | untested |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinal-BPR (default) | 3.39 | 0.93 | −5.91 |
| Ordinal-BPR [`lambda_ord`=0] | 3.60 | −0.76 | −3.75 |
| Ordinal-BPR [`hard_neg_ratio`=0] | 2.83 | 2.47 | −4.92 |
| BPR | 4.31 | 0.05 | −3.90 |

**Mechanism.**
- The ordinal term enforces score(neutral) > score(untested), as the Methods describe. In leave-one-out, the withheld consumed compound is untested during training.
- Default Ordinal-BPR also draws its soft negatives only from untested compounds, so the withheld compound is sampled as a negative more often than in plain BPR.
- Both effects push the withheld compound towards the bottom tier, below the measured neutrals it is then compared with in A1. A1 is therefore structurally biased against Ordinal-BPR.
- Protocol B removes this asymmetry: withheld consumed and withheld neutral pairs are both untested.

**The ordinal term still costs 0.04 in B** (0.865 → 0.827 at dim 32). The hard negatives are the useful component: without them,
neutral compounds are never shown to the model as negatives and score almost as high as consumed ones.

**Docstring error.** The `OrdinalBPRModel` docstring (`core/models.py:416`) says the term makes neutral compounds score *below*
unobserved ones. The loss and the scores above show the opposite. The manuscript Methods state the direction correctly.

## 8. Implications for the revision

- **Retract** "Ordinal-BPR maintained stable predictive power across the entire range (AUC of 0.94)". That curve is plain BPR
  evaluated on the mixed pool.
- **Describe the benchmark that matches the text.** The Results describe a consumed-vs-neutral comparison. Protocol B is the only
  version that treats all models symmetrically and matches deployment, where every scored pair is untested. Under B, the BPR
  family is best at ≥ 16 dimensions; at dim 32, Ordinal-BPR scores 0.827 ± 0.009 and BPR 0.824 ± 0.006.
  WR-MF and SVD are within 0.01 of them at dims 4–8.
- **Reconsider the choice of Ordinal-BPR at 32 dimensions.** Under B, Ordinal-BPR peaks at 16 dimensions (0.854). The `lambda_ord = 0`
  variant (hard negatives only) is best at dim 32 on A1 and B. It was tested at dim 32 only; a full dimension sweep is needed before
  adopting it.
- **Fig. S3 is unaffected.** The held-out-isolate validation uses genuine Ordinal-BPR with known negatives.

## 9. Caveats

- **176 pairs with both neutral and secreted rows** are labelled secreted here and never used as negatives in evaluation. They remain in
  Ordinal-BPR's training known negatives, as passed through by `prepare_training_data`.
- **The untested pool (A2) is easy for every model** (0.92–0.996 at dim 32). Untested compounds lie outside the microbe's measured
  panel; the source datasets profile different compound panels. A2 therefore mostly tests panel membership and is a weak test of substrate preference.
- **Pooled metrics mix between-microbe score scales.** Per-microbe ROC-AUC is the quantity that corresponds to the Results claim.
- **Ablations were run at dim 32 only.**
